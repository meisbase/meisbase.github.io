Mei-Yu Kuo ("Mei," pronounced as "May") is a sociologist studying gender, work, and migration. She is broadly interested in gender inequality in contemporary societies and how social institutions interact with gender systems to produce divergent trajectories in work, family, and other life chances. One of her forthcoming papers in Research in Social Stratification and Mobility, based on her second-year project, examines how gendered college major choices contribute to differential labor force integration across genders and introduce wage disparities—an angle largely neglected by previous studies on gendered employment dynamics. Another current project investigates how married women's economic contributions and status within marriage change across cohorts, and how such historical changes can be explained by cohort differences or the strengthening of educational stratification processes. A third project in this strand explores how college pathways, particularly the timing of first attending college, shape union formation for young women and men and the implications for individualization and the underlying stratifying family processes.

The second strand of her research focuses on migration, assimilation, and integration into U.S. society, and how existing or emerging social institutions shape these trajectories. She is currently working on two projects examining the health outcomes of immigrants and how these may be negatively assimilated relative to U.S. natives’ health. In the future, she plans to explore how immigrants assimilate into the U.S. through gendered and cultural processes and the implications for their economic and health well-being.

She is currently a Sociology PhD student at The Ohio State University. Mei obtained her M.A. in Sociology from National Taiwan University and her B.A. in Journalism from National Chengchi University. She welcomes contact via email at kuo.355@buckeyemail.osu.edu.
